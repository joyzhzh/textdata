Proposed TextData adaptation

From documented language to a bounded comparison

The initial target is described functional differentiation relative to a defined earlier corpus. A different phrase, a rare word or a distant vector is evidence about a representation. The substantive interpretation requires a connection to the function being studied.

01 / ACQUIRE

Retain the textual record

Keep source identities, entity relationships, retrieval dates and text versions. An earlier post date does not prove that wording was available then.

02 / ANNOTATE

Extract supported functions

Record actors, tasks, functions and conditions with supporting passages, explicit unknowns and the annotation version. This produces observations, not a novelty conclusion.

03 / REPRESENT

Choose what to compare

Compare word weights, topics, embeddings or structured concepts and relationships. Preserve the feature definitions and preprocessing choices.

04 / DEFINE REFERENCES

Freeze the comparison population

Specify the earlier eligible corpus, time window, exclusions and entity/version rules. Retain the actual retrieved predecessors and coverage limits.

05 / MEASURE

Keep interpretations separate

Nearest distance, mean distance, rarity, recombination and a grounded judgment answer different questions. Preserve matched functions, residual differences and uncertainty.

06 / VALIDATE

Test the intended interpretation

Assess extraction, retrieval and comparison separately. Use human criteria, meaningful perturbations and separated calibration/testing samples before substantive inference.

Fictional illustration · no scores or model outputs

New wording or an additional function?

Earlier description

Schedules appointments from a shared calendar.

Meaning-preserving wording change

Books meetings using a team calendar.

Focal description

Schedules appointments and routes exceptions to a supervisor for approval.

The focal sentence adds a stated approval relationship. This observation does not establish an actual working capability or market firstness. An eligible predecessor describing the same relationship would change the bounded comparison.
