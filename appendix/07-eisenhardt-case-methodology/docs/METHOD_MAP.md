# The Eisenhardt method, mapped

A methodological reference for preparing case-based theory building. Read the eight phases alongside the cited sources. This is a bounded synthesis, not a systematic review or a validated analytical engine.

The source notes are in `method-map/sources.json`; the phase map is in `method-map/map.json`. Some entries describe the same work, so source rows are not a count of distinct publications.

## The method, phase by phase

| Phase | What the method asks | What it leaves to inspect | A common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Define the puzzle | Ask a consequential question with little, conflicting, or incomplete theory/evidence; do not pre-specify the relationships to be discovered. | Research question, initial construct sensitizers, contribution target, prior-theory proximity. | Declaring a generic “gap” while implicitly fixing the answer in advance. |
| 2. Design theoretical variation | Choose cases because the phenomenon is present and because similarities/differences can reveal or challenge theoretical patterns. Designs can include matched pairs, polar types, racing cases, and embedded replications. | Case-selection matrix, inclusion logic, expected replication/contrast, alternative-explanation controls. | Convenience sampling relabeled as theoretical sampling. |
| 3. Build case evidence | Combine sources appropriate to the question; qualitative, quantitative, archival, observational, or mixed evidence are all permitted. Preserve chronology and provenance. | Case database, source/provenance map, event chronology, construct-evidence table. | Treating interview quotations as the only legitimate data, or losing source-level traceability. |
| 4. Analyze each case as a case | Establish the internal story, event sequence, constructs, and candidate relationships before pooling attention across cases. | Within-case narrative/chronology, provisional constructs, disconfirming evidence, confidence notes. | Jumping directly to themes across interviews and erasing case context. |
| 5. Compare across cases | Use multiple lenses and deliberate pairings; examine replication, contradiction, presence/absence, sequence, equifinality, and qualitative differences. | Cross-case matrix, replication/contradiction ledger, revised construct definitions. | Counting surface similarities without asking why patterns occur. |
| 6. Build mechanisms and theory | Link constructs through explicit theoretical arguments, test rival explanations, and state boundary conditions. | Parsimonious model, propositions where useful, mechanism ledger, rival-explanation table, boundary conditions. | Presenting a category taxonomy or data display as if it were explanatory theory. |
| 7. Iterate with data and literature | Revisit cases and relevant literatures to sharpen constructs, reveal novelty, improve internal validity, and locate contribution; theory elaboration/abduction is allowed. | Theory–evidence revisions, literature contrast table, negative-case responses. | Claiming a literal blank slate or adding citations only after analysis as decoration. |
| 8. Stop and communicate | Stop when additional evidence no longer materially improves the theoretical account, while also acknowledging pragmatic limits; justify case count rather than invoke a magic number. | Saturation/stopping memo, residual uncertainty, testable implications, transparent methods narrative. | Treating “4–10 cases” or “about eight” as a rule. |

## Core features and folklore

Core features supported by the current first-party clarification:

- Theory building is the organizing aim; constructs alone are insufficient without relationships and why-mechanisms.
- Cases are theoretical replications and analytic units, not observations pooled under statistical sampling logic.
- Careful case selection creates theoretically useful similarity and difference and helps address alternative explanations.
- Constant comparison, within-case analysis, cross-case analysis, literature iteration, boundary conditions, and theoretical arguments work as one system.
- The desired theory balances parsimony, empirical accuracy, logical coherence, testability, and relevance beyond the immediate setting.

Not method requirements:

- A fixed count of cases. Four to ten is a historically common range, not a rule.
- Interviews or qualitative data only. Archival, quantitative, observational, ethnographic, and mixed evidence may be appropriate.
- Performance as the outcome.
- Variance theory rather than process theory.
- A rigid positivist philosophy or a fully detached researcher.
- A standardized visual data structure analogous to the Gioia display.

## Method demarcation

| Approach | Primary analytic target | Distinctive warrant | Relationship to Eisenhardt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eisenhardt | Explanatory theory: constructs, relationships, mechanisms, and boundaries across cases | Theoretical sampling plus within/cross-case constant comparison and replication logic | Focal method. |
| Gioia | Informant-grounded concepts and a transparent progression from first-order terms to second-order themes and a grounded model | Informant-centric coding, data structure, and traceable movement from participant terms to researcher concepts | Shares induction and iteration, but foregrounds informant meaning and coding architecture; it should not be treated as the same workflow. |
| Yin | Case-study design, evidence convergence, and analytic replication for explanatory or deductive as well as exploratory studies | Case protocol, multiple sources, replication logic, chain of evidence | Supplies much of the case and replication logic that Eisenhardt integrates into inductive theory building. |
| Grounded theory | Theory generated through theoretical sampling, constant comparison, and saturation | Iterative category/theory development grounded in data | Supplies core inductive logic; Eisenhardt adds a particularly explicit multi-case comparison and theoretical-argument architecture. |
| Langley/process research | Explanations of how phenomena unfold over time using multiple process-theorizing strategies | Temporal patterning, sequencing, narrative/visual/bracketing and related strategies | Complementary rather than categorically opposed; Eisenhardt 2021 explicitly embraces time and process possibilities. |

Combining techniques is defensible only when the research question, assumptions, unit of analysis, and output logic remain coherent. A paper that cites all approaches but merely borrows their visible reporting devices has not established method fit.

## Development beyond preparation

The following analytical tasks remain for the researcher. TextData has not implemented or evaluated these capabilities:

1. theoretical case-selection decisions
2. within-case event chronologies and evidence provenance
3. construct definitions and measure evolution
4. cross-case replication, contradiction, and negative-case tests
5. mechanism and rival-explanation reasoning
6. boundary conditions and theoretical generalizability
7. theory–data–literature iteration
8. stopping/saturation decisions

## The sources behind the map

Source notes state the evidence type, confidence and material limits of each interpretation. Consult the cited work before using a claim.

### W001 Eisenhardt (1989), Building Theories from Case Study Research

Cells: C1, C2, C4. DOI 10.5465/amr.1989.4308385.

- The foundational article presents a staged but iterative process for inducing theory from case studies, from specifying research questions through reaching closure; it identifies within-case analysis and replication logic as distinctive features and positions the approach for new topic areas. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)

### W002 Eisenhardt & Graebner (2007), Theory Building From Cases: Opportunities and Challenges

Cells: C1, C2, C4. DOI 10.5465/amj.2007.24160888.

- Theory building from cases uses one or more cases to create constructs, propositions, or midrange theory from case-based evidence; under replication logic each case is treated as a distinct experiment and analytic unit rather than pooled as a sample data point. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)
- The 2007 refinement frames precise wording and thoughtful research design as responses to recurring publication and evaluation challenges for theory-building case research. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)

### W003 Eisenhardt (2021), What is the Eisenhardt Method, really?

Cells: C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6, C8. DOI 10.1177/1476127020982866.

- The method's singular organizing aim is theory building—especially from multiple cases and theoretical logic—with defining features including non-obvious research questions, deliberate case selection, clearly identified constructs and relationships, theoretical mechanisms, and boundary conditions. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)
- Theoretical sampling selects cases where the focal phenomenon is likely and designs similarities and differences across cases to sharpen focus, reduce alternative explanations, and improve theoretical generalizability; designs may include matched pairs, racing cases, and polar types. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)
- Analysis combines constant comparison between emergent theory and data, treating cases as standalone replications, systematic within- and cross-case comparison, and explicit development of why-theoretical arguments rather than pattern description alone. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)
- A fixed data type, a fixed number of cases, performance as a necessary outcome, and a variance-only theory form are not defining requirements; 4–10 cases are common but not a rule, and single cases with embedded replication may also support the logic. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)
- Eisenhardt's later position is ontologically and epistemologically flexible: she retains an objective-reality commitment while recognizing social construction, unavoidable researcher influence, and the art involved in constructing concepts and relationships. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)
- The 2021 refresh broadens the method beyond a blank-slate reading to theory elaboration/abduction, calls for greater attention to time, and proposes complementarity with machine learning; it does not itself provide a reusable computational implementation. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)

### W004 Gehman et al. (2018), Finding Theory–Method Fit: A Comparison of Three Qualitative Approaches to Theory Building

Cells: C2, C3, C5. DOI 10.1177/1056492617706029.

- Qualitative theory-building approaches are heterogeneous and can embody different ontological and epistemological assumptions, so authors should establish theory–method fit rather than cite Eisenhardt, Gioia, Langley, Yin, and grounded-theory authorities as if interchangeable. (Evidence: Independent synthesis or critique; confidence: high.)
- In Eisenhardt's own account, theory building from cases combines grounded-theory openness and constant comparison with Yin-style case and replication logic; not all qualitative research uses cases and not all case research builds theory. (Evidence: Primary method text; confidence: high.)

### W005 Gioia et al. (2022), A Curated Debate: On Using Templates in Qualitative Research

### W006 Purposeful sampling and saturation in qualitative research methodologies: recommendations and review

(Evidence: Independent synthesis or critique; confidence: moderate.)

### W007 Ridder (2017), The theory contribution of case study research designs

Cells: C2, C3, C5, C6. DOI 10.1007/s40685-017-0045-z.

- Comparative case-method literature places Eisenhardt on an inductive theory-building axis that prioritizes constructs and their relationships, and records a long-running trade-off critique: comparative rigor may come at the cost of rich, context-sensitive stories. (Evidence: Independent synthesis or critique; confidence: moderate.)
- The method's operational sequence includes tentative constructs without preset relationships, theoretical case selection, triangulated qualitative and quantitative evidence where useful, within- and cross-case pattern analysis, iteration between evidence and theory, and refinement through replication logic and related literature. (Evidence: Independent synthesis or critique; confidence: moderate.)
- Published case studies often cite canonical methodology without following it consistently or articulating their theory purpose, narrowing case research to generic exploratory claims and weakening methodological transparency. (Evidence: Independent synthesis or critique; confidence: moderate.)
