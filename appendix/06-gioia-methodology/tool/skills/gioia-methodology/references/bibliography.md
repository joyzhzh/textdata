# Selected methodological references

These references orient readers to the methodology and its debates. They do not validate this software or endorse AI-generated interpretations. Entries are bibliographic pointers, not a claim that every historical full text was inspected. Publication metadata was checked against publisher records on 2026-09-20; issue years are used where online-first years differ.

## Core methodology

1. Gioia, D. A., Corley, K. G., & Hamilton, A. L. (2013). Seeking qualitative rigor in inductive research: Notes on the Gioia methodology. *Organizational Research Methods, 16*(1), 15–31. [https://doi.org/10.1177/1094428112452151](https://doi.org/10.1177/1094428112452151)

   The starting point for the skill's distinction between informant-oriented expressions, researcher concepts, and a traceable data structure. The agent instructions are an implementation choice, not a reproduction of the paper.

2. Gioia, D. (2021). A systematic methodology for doing qualitative research. *The Journal of Applied Behavioral Science, 57*(1), 20–29. [https://doi.org/10.1177/0021886320982715](https://doi.org/10.1177/0021886320982715)

   A later methodological account. Read it in its own context rather than treating a later account as proof of every earlier study's procedures.

## Theory–method fit, process, and templates

3. Gehman, J., Glaser, V. L., Eisenhardt, K. M., Gioia, D., Langley, A., & Corley, K. G. (2018). Finding theory–method fit: A comparison of three qualitative approaches to theory building. *Journal of Management Inquiry, 27*(3), 284–300. [https://doi.org/10.1177/1056492617706029](https://doi.org/10.1177/1056492617706029)

   A comparison that helps readers assess which approach fits their question and assumptions. This skill does not turn distinct approaches into one universal procedure.

4. Gioia, D., Corley, K., Eisenhardt, K., Feldman, M., Langley, A., Lê, J., Golden-Biddle, K., Locke, K., Mees-Buss, J., Piekkari, R., Ravasi, D., Rerup, C., Schmid, T., Silverman, D., & Welch, C. (2022). A curated debate: On using “templates” in qualitative research. *Journal of Management Inquiry, 31*(3), 231–252. [https://doi.org/10.1177/10564926221098955](https://doi.org/10.1177/10564926221098955)

   Contrasting positions on templates. The working records in this package are optional aids, not a claim that filling their fields makes an analysis rigorous.

5. Langley, A. (1999). Strategies for theorizing from process data. *Academy of Management Review, 24*(4), 691–710. [https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1999.2553248](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.1999.2553248)

   Further reading for process analysis and the relationship between data and theory. The skill asks for explicit support for proposed temporal and causal links.

## Historical empirical exemplars

6. Gioia, D. A., & Chittipeddi, K. (1991). Sensemaking and sensegiving in strategic change initiation. *Strategic Management Journal, 12*(6), 433–448. [https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.4250120604](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.4250120604)

7. Corley, K. G., & Gioia, D. A. (2004). Identity ambiguity and change in the wake of a corporate spin-off. *Administrative Science Quarterly, 49*(2), 173–208. [https://doi.org/10.2307/4131471](https://doi.org/10.2307/4131471)

These two studies are reading pointers, not datasets for this repository. No claim here reconstructs their full analytic procedures from metadata or abstracts.

## Software-specific choices

Exact-byte matching, hashes, JSON statuses, permission gates, preservation rules, and adoption records are this project's engineering choices. The references above are not evidence that those mechanisms improve qualitative interpretation. All bundled examples and tests are synthetic; the repository redistributes no article full text or participant data.
